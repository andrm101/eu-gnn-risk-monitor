conflict_density: which events count as "conflict"?

The ACLED file has four disorder types. Choosing scope changes what the GNN learns to associate with risk.

A

Political Violence only (Recommended)

23,332 rows — Battles, explosions, violence against civilians, riots with casualties.

Pros

  • Closest to the intuitive meaning of "conflict risk" — physical harm events
  • Methodologically clean: matches UCDP/GED definition of organized violence
  • Signal is meaningful: EU regions normally have very low values, spikes are real
  • Defensible in methods section as "physical conflict exposure"

Cons

  • Misses social instability signals (strikes, large protests) that precede violence
  • Low variance for most Western EU regions (many near-zero values)

Key 2022 spike: Ukraine war border events inflate PL, RO, SK, HU readings — exactly the signal we want CEI to capture.

B

Political Violence + Demonstrations

~106,000 rows — Adds protests, strikes, peaceful and violent demonstrations.

Pros

  • Captures social unrest that precedes violence (Yellow Vests → Gilets jaunes → actual riots)
  • More variance across Western EU (France, Germany have high protest activity)
  • Richer leading-indicator signal for GNN

Cons

  • France and Germany dominate the signal — 60%+ of protest rows are these two countries
  • Conflates "vibrant democracy" with "instability" — Paris protests ≠ Kyiv shelling
  • Harder to explain in a thesis without explicit weighting scheme

If choosing B, would weight political violence events 3× vs. demonstrations to preserve signal hierarchy.

Data note — regardless of choice

  • Coverage: 2018–2023 only (6 years). conflict_density will be NaN in the panel for 2010–2017.
  • ADMIN1 → NUTS2: centroid spatial join via geopandas. France/Germany ADMIN1 units are NUTS1-sized → multiple NUTS2 get the same value per parent region.
  • Normalization: events / panel population × 100,000 (not ACLED's POPULATION_EXPOSURE).
